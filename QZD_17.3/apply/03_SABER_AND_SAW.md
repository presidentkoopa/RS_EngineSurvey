# PART ONE — THE SAW (RS_ShieldSawQ)

## 1.1 What the mod does today

**Three states, one event handler.** `SSQ_Handler : EventHandler` (`state.zs:21`, registered in
`ShieldSaw/MAPINFO.txt`) holds `SS_STOWED / SS_DRAWN / SS_FLYING` (`state.zs:23`) plus per-player
pose, props, lock lists and loadout memory (`state.zs:25-53`). All decisions run once a tic in
`WorldTick` (`state.zs:314-374`) on every machine.

**The pose is synced, and that is the mod's spine.** Stock QZD writes `OffhandPos`/`OffhandAngle`
on the console player's pawn only, from the renderer. So `SendPose()` (`state.zs:1020-1043`) reads
those fields for the local player, packs hand position to quarter-units and the three angles to
10 bits each (`pose.zs:181-203`), and sends `ssq-pose`; `NetworkProcess` (`state.zs:959-965`)
unpacks it into an 8-deep ring buffer on **every** peer. **The sim reads only the history, on the
sender's machine too** (`pose.zs:1-13`). Hand velocity is differenced in the **body** frame and
turned back into world space (`pose.zs:73-91`), so walking and snap-turning do not read as arm
motion — with the measured numbers for why at `pose.zs:50-69`.

**The shield blocks by geometry, not by collision.** There is **no plate actor anywhere in the
mod.** The plate is a **thick disc** — centre, face normal, radius, half-thickness — computed per
tic from the synced pose (`guard.zs:80-107`), and the block is a segment-vs-disc test (`Crosses()`,
`guard.zs:139-173`), resolved in `Inventory.AbsorbDamage` (`guard.zs:210-317`). That classifies the
incoming damage into four kinds — missile, hitscan, melee, blast (`guard.zs:220-249`) —
reconstructs the attack's line and tests it. Blocked means `newdamage = 0` (`:262`), a spark at the
crossing point, and a return.

The geometry is **measured off the mesh**, not guessed: `guard.zs:34-68` follows `shield.md3`
through `models_md3.cpp BuildVertexBuffer`, `models.cpp RenderHUDModel` and
`hw_vrmodes.cpp MapWeaponDir` to `F = mx−6, L = ±my, U = mz−5`, and lists per-submesh centres and
radii. `ssq_plate_f/-l/-u` default to those numbers (`guard.zs:87-89`).

**Three interception paths, not one.**
- **Contact block** — `AbsorbDamage` as above. Catches everything that reaches you.
- **The sweep** — `DoEffect` (`guard.zs:456-494`) walks `SSQ_Handler.mMissiles`, built in
  `WorldThingSpawned` (`state.zs:293-297`), and turns anything whose path from one tic back to
  `ssq_sweep_lead` (3) tics ahead crosses the plate — **including shots that would have missed**.
  It uses that list rather than a `BlockThingsIterator` because a projectile is `+NOBLOCKMAP`,
  which the mod measured (`state.zs:287-291`).
- **The muzzle stop** — `WorldHitscanPreFired` (`state.zs:892-927`) → `StopsHitscan`
  (`guard.zs:352-439`), cancelling a bullet **before the engine traces it**.

**How shots go back.** One aim rule for all paths, `DeflectDir` (`guard.zs:532-553`):
`ssq_deflect_aim` 0 = at the shooter, **1 = where you are looking** (default), helped onto the
nearest enemy within `ssq_deflect_assist` 14° of gaze via `GazeTarget` (`guard.zs:499-525`),
2 = mirrored off the face. A swept missile is **turned in place** — new velocity, `target = mo`,
`master =` the saw — not replaced (`BatBack`, `guard.zs:558-586`). A missile blocked on contact is
already mid-impact, so that path spawns a fresh one and zeroes the original (`Deflect`,
`guard.zs:590-605`). A stopped bullet returns as a `LineAttack` fired by an `SSQ_Ricochet` helper
(`guard.zs:412-435`) — **not** by the player, because a player's `LineAttack` in VR is re-aimed
down the main controller (`guard.zs:406-411`; correct — see §1.3).

**Perfect block.** `guard.zs:266-279`: the plate must have been moving **into** the attack at
`ssq_perfect_speed` (25 u/s) along the incoming line at the moment of contact —
`(pose.Vel() * TICRATE) dot outward >= need`. It pays 60% faster return and a guaranteed stagger,
and is signalled without text: higher tone, double-length buzz, white bloom at the contact
(`guard.zs:290-300`).

**The bash and the grind.** `SSQ_Guard.HeldTick` (`guard.zs:608-714`), called once a tic from the
shield's Ready state (`shield.zs:149`). `shove = pose.Vel()*TICRATE dot n`; the push is also
**integrated** as a decaying sum (`pushPath`, `guard.zs:632-635`) so a steady lean counts, not only
a snap — `ssq_bash_sustain` 0.6. Over `ssq_bash_speed` 45 u/s, a `BlockThingsIterator` finds
victims, tests them against the disc widened by `ssq_bash_reach` 1.2, requires `CheckSight`
(`:669`), damages, and shoves with knockback scaled by `100/Mass` (`:702-706`). The grind is the
rim actually on them, every `ssq_grind_every` 4 tics (`:621`, `:672-682`).

**The disc.** `SSQ_Disc : Actor`, `Projectile +RIPPER +BOUNCEONWALLS` (`disc.zs:43-71`). Thrown
from the **peak** 2-tic window of the last six pose samples, not the release tic
(`pose.zs:110-124`), with the `pixelstretch` term put back into the magnitude only (`:105-109`).
The throw must be forward (`ssq_throw_forward` 0.35 ≈ 70°, `state.zs:414-420`). It visits up to
`ssq_locks` 5 targets painted one at a time while held (`LocksTick`, `state.zs:576-670`), winds up
toward `ssq_cruise_max` while it has somewhere to be (`disc.zs:302-308`), and returns along
**breadcrumbs** dropped on the way out (`disc.zs:383-394`) so it never comes home through a wall,
with a patience cap after which it goes `+NOCLIP` (`disc.zs:369-373`). Hitting is tiered by
`SpawnHealth` in `SpecialMissileHit` (`disc.zs:469-542`): fodder gibbed with `DMG_FORCED`, mid-tier
embedded and ground, heavy turns it away.

**What holds it together.** `state.zs` owns the loadout fight. `PinHands` (`state.zs:462-514`) puts
the shield back if anything takes the off hand, writing `PendingWeapon` rather than the slot so
**load order stops mattering** (`:478-484`), with the engine's own `switchhand` semantics parsed out
at `:436-449`. `IsFiller` (`state.zs:183-188`) distinguishes a weapon that put *itself* in an empty
off hand (the pack's hands, the saber's off blade) from the player actually taking the hand away.

## 1.2 The plate question — the mod has no plate, and that is the right answer

`07_SHIELDS_PARRY.md` §3.2-§4.2 designs a `+SOLID` plate actor moved with `SetOrigin(pos, true)`
every tic. **The saw has no such actor.** Every question in the brief about it is therefore moot,
and I will not invent work:

- **Is it using the right move call?** It makes no move call. There is nothing to interpolate.
- **Is its plate sized for a square?** Its plate is not a box. It is a disc with a face normal
  (`guard.zs:139-173`) — the shape `07` §5 wall 1 says the engine cannot represent. The mod gets it
  by never asking the engine.
- **One actor or several?** Zero.

**And the reason is a correction to `07`.** `guard.zs:3-9`: a hand-held plate at guard pose sits
*inside the player's own collision box*, and the engine resolves the missile against whichever
candidate `PIT_CheckThing` reaches first — there is **no distance ordering** in that iteration, and
the blocking test is a flat Chebyshev overlap (`src/playsim/p_map.cpp:1417-1419`; block/no-damage
branch `:1677-1680`). The player is radius 16; a hand 30-50 cm forward is 10-17 map units out. So
the archetypal pose — shield up in front of your chest — is exactly where a `+SOLID` plate stops
being reachable. The mod credits this to its own 2026-10-17 audit; the engine lines support it.
**`07` §4.1 should carry that caveat.**

Costs of the chosen design, stated honestly:
- `07` §3.2's one-tic plate lag **does not exist here** — the geometry is recomputed from this
  tic's pose, so the test is never stale.
- `07` §3.1's "the plate sweeps nothing" is answered directly by `PlateTravel()`
  (`guard.zs:326-335`) on both block paths.
- The price is that a blocked projectile must be re-created rather than passed through
  (`guard.zs:590-605`, `missile.vel = (0,0,0)` at `:604`). **The saber solved this better** — §2.5.
  That is the one directly borrowable idea between the halves.

**Verdict: EXISTS, shipped and sound. ALREADY RIGHT. No work.**

### The one measurement worth taking: `ssq_plate_thick`

`ssq_plate_thick` defaults to **25.0 cm** (`ShieldSaw/CVARINFO.txt:102`) and `Crosses()` accepts
anything within `±thick` of the face (`guard.zs:154`). Against `ssq_plate_radius` 29.2 cm that
makes the guard volume **58 cm across and 50 cm deep** — very nearly a cube, not a plate. The
thickness exists for a real reason (a shot skimming the face edge-on, `guard.zs:133-136`), but
50 cm is a hand's length of slack on both sides, where the saber's equivalent is
`ubqs_blade_guard 6.0` units ≈ 17 cm (`QuestSaber/CVARINFO:65`). This may be exactly what makes the
saw feel generous and the saber precise — which may be correct for a shield. **It is a measurement
for the owner in the headset, not a bug, and I am not proposing a number.** `ssq_debug_ring` draws
the rim and the face spoke (`guard.zs:717-735`) but not the thickness; making it draw the slab is
**BUILDABLE / S** and would settle it in one session.

## 1.4 Parry refinement

**What the mod uses: none of the engine's parry machinery, and it should stay that way.**

No `+REFLECTIVE`, no `+SHIELDREFLECT`, no `+AIMREFLECT`, no `ReflectOffActor` anywhere in the saw.
Instead the perfect block (`guard.zs:266-279`) is a timed directional parry keyed on the plate's
measured velocity into the attack, with the turn done by writing `vel`/`angle`/`pitch` directly
(`guard.zs:572-574`).

**Why that is better here** — see §2.4 for the full argument and the lines. In short:
`+SHIELDREFLECT` is a ±45° *gate* keyed on the blocker's body yaw, and inside the gate the missile
is deflected by a **random** ±45° off its own heading via a playsim RNG draw
(`src/playsim/p_mobj.cpp:3468-3477`, `pr_reflect()` at `:3474`). It does **not** aim at the shooter
without `+AIMREFLECT` as well (`:3491-3502`).

**What a tighter window would buy.** The brief asks what 4-8 tics would buy against the Centaur's
45 (`wadsrc/static/zscript/actors/hexen/centaur.zs:34-39`, verified). The honest answer: **the saw
has no window to tighten.** Its parry is not a window — it is an instantaneous speed test on the
tic of contact (`guard.zs:278`), which is *tighter than 4 tics* and needs no latch and no cooldown.
The Centaur comparison does not apply, and converting it to a window would be a regression.

**The real refinement available, and it is small.** The perfect test reads `pose.Vel()`, the single
last-two-samples difference (`pose.zs:87-91`). A swat's peak is often one tic before contact —
exactly the problem `ThrowVel` (`pose.zs:110-124`) already solves for the throw by taking the
fastest 2-tic window of the last six. Reusing `VelAt(k)` (`pose.zs:94-98`) over a 2-3 sample window
would stop a genuinely hard parry reading as an ordinary block because the arm had begun to slow.
**BUILDABLE / S.**

**Labels:** parry as shipped **ALREADY RIGHT**; `+SHIELDREFLECT` **WALL, contraindicated**;
peak-window perfect test **BUILDABLE / S**.

## 1.5 The disc as a swung blade — the one real gap

Two different things, opposite verdicts.

**The thrown disc: already safe.** A `Projectile` with `Radius 10` (`disc.zs:63`), so
`P_XYMovement` substeps it at `maxmove = radius−1 = 9` units with a full `TryMove` each
(`src/playsim/p_mobj.cpp:2166-2192`) — the engine sweeps it and it cannot tunnel. `+RIPPER` plus
`SpecialMissileHit` (`disc.zs:469`) gives per-victim resolution with a 6-tic same-target lockout
(`:475`). **EXISTS. No work.**

**The held saw's edge: it samples, and it is the only unswept contact in the mod.** This is the
answer to the brief's question, and it is a genuine finding.

`HeldTick` computes **one** plate centre `C` (`guard.zs:614`) and tests every victim against it
(`guard.zs:650-671`). There is no `PlateTravel()` in that loop, although the function is in the
same class and both block paths use it. Consequences:

- At the bash threshold itself — `ssq_bash_speed` 45 u/s = **1.29 units/tic** — the gap is
  negligible. The bash is safe by construction: a slow bash is not a bash.
- The **grind** is the exposure. It is the rim held against a body, fired only every
  `ssq_grind_every` 4 tics (`guard.zs:621`), so the rim is sampled at **8.75 Hz**. Sweep the arm
  along a line of bodies at a realistic 3 m/s (`07` §3.1: 2.91 u/tic) and the plate advances
  ~11.6 units between grind evaluations — against a `t.radius + 10` depth window (`:661`) and a
  29 cm (≈9.9 unit) rim. Bodies in the gap are skipped. This is precisely `01_MELEE.md` §3.5's
  failure mode, and precisely what `guard.zs:319-325`'s own comment says was fixed *for the block*
  and not for this.

**The fix, in the mod's own idiom.** `01_MELEE.md` §3.5 gives the swept chord; the saw needs no
`LineTrace` for it, because it already has the better shape — reuse the three-sample walk from
`guard.zs:484-488`:

```
Vector3 travel = g.PlateTravel();
for (int s = 0; s <= 2 && !touched; s++) {
    Vector3 Cs = C + travel * (1.0 - s * 0.5);
    // the existing inplane / along test, against Cs
}
```

**Trace cost per tic, as the brief asks.** This adds **no traces at all** — two extra vector
subtractions and dot products per candidate, inside a `BlockThingsIterator` that already runs. For
comparison, done with `01_MELEE` §3.5's `LineTrace` form instead: 3 sub-chords × 1 hand =
**3 `LineTrace` calls per tic ≈ 105 traces/s**, which `01_MELEE` §3.4 prices as "nothing" (one
`FPathTraverse` each, the same order as a bullet). Either is free; the iterator form is cheaper and
matches the code around it.

Second, independent half of the same fix: change `ssq_grind_every` from a **tic gate** to a
**per-victim** cooldown, which is how the saber caps its own rate (`ubqs_cut_rate` 8,
`QuestSaber/zscript.txt:1873`, `:1893`) and how the disc caps its embed grind (`disc.zs:475`). The
grind would then evaluate every tic — catching what the sweep crosses — while each body still takes
damage at the same rate. **BUILDABLE / S**, and the better of the two.

**Label: BUILDABLE / S. The saw's highest-value refinement.**

## 2.2 Swing arc tracing — it sweeps. The residual is the arc, not the sweep.

**The brief asks whether it sweeps, and says not to infer. I can cite it.**

`CutLine`, `:1894-1921`, with the reason in its own comment: *"A 90-unit blade crosses a 20-radius
imp in well under a tic on a hard swing, so both samples could sit either side of the body and the
swing passed clean through it — the harder you swung, the more it missed."* Three blade positions
per tic (`sk = 2,1,0` → `f = 1.0, 0.5, 0.0`), each a full segment-vs-victim test (`:1911-1917`).
`SweptSep` (`:2451-2469`) and `Deflect` (`:2086-2095`) do the same. `01_MELEE.md` §3.5's problem is
solved, and was solved from measurement.

**The residual, and it is the saber's highest-value refinement: the sweep is a LINEAR lerp, and a
sword swing is an arc.**

`:1909-1910` interpolates base and tip independently and linearly:
```
Vector3 sb = pb + (base - pb) * f;
Vector3 se = pt + ((base + dir * len) - pt) * f;
```
A blade rotating about the wrist does not travel along that chord — it **bows outside it**.
`01_MELEE.md` §3.5 names the number: sagitta `= len · (1 − cos(θ/2))`. For the saber's own stated
90-unit blade turning 60° in a tic that is **`90 · (1 − cos 30°) ≈ 12.1 units`**, against
`ubqs_blade_guard 6.0` (`CVARINFO:65`) and `ubqs_cut_pad 3.0` (`CVARINFO:102`) — **the bulge is
larger than the tolerance**, so a target sitting mid-arc can fall between all three samples. Same
class of bug the mod already fixed once; it fixed it with three straight lines instead of three
points.

**The fix, exactly as `01_MELEE.md` §3.5 prescribes.** Interpolate the **angles**, not the
endpoints: lerp `(HA(), HP(), HR())` (`:826-828`) across the tic on the shortest arc
(`a0 + Actor.deltaangle(a0, a1) * t`, because `AttackAngle`/`OffhandAngle` wrap) and rebuild base
and tip from `BladeDirOf` at each sub-step. Everything needed is already stored — `prevTip`,
`prevBase`, `lastTip`, `lastBase` (`:418-425`) — and `BladeDirOf` is already a pure function of
`(ha, hp, hr, frame, off)`, so this is a loop body, not a redesign.

**Cost.** 3 samples → 5 above a speed threshold, on two legs when `back` is on (`:1858`): **at worst
10 segment tests per tic per saber**, all arithmetic, inside an iterator that already runs. Done as
`LineTrace` instead, `01_MELEE` §3.4 prices a two-handed sword at **6 traces per tic ≈ 210/s** and
calls it "still nothing". The segment form is cheaper still.

Cheaper half: make the sample count adaptive. `ubqs_trail_steps` already ramps the trail by swing
speed on exactly this principle (`:1797-1800`); the cut can read the same speed and do the same.

**Label: BUILDABLE / S-M (S for adaptive count, M for angular lerp). The saber's highest-value
refinement.**

## 2.4 Correction: `+SHIELDREFLECT` is worse than `07_SHIELDS_PARRY.md` says

This matters to both halves, so it is stated once, here, with the lines I read.

`07` §2.2 and §4.4 present `+SHIELDREFLECT` as a ±45° frontal cone within which a missile is
"re-aimed at its own shooter at full `Speed`". **The second half is wrong unless `+AIMREFLECT` is
also set.** `AActor::AdjustReflectionAngle`, `src/playsim/p_mobj.cpp:3468-3477`, read in full:

```
if (thing->flags4 & MF4_SHIELDREFLECT)
{
    if ((flags7 & MF7_NOSHIELDREFLECT) || absangle(angle, thing->Angles.Yaw) > DAngle::fromDeg(45))
        return true;                      // let the missile explode
    if (pr_reflect() < 128) angle += DAngle::fromDeg(45);
    else                    angle -= DAngle::fromDeg(45);
}
```

Inside the cone the missile is turned by a **random ±45° off its own heading**, chosen by a
**playsim RNG draw**. The re-aim at the shooter is a separate additive branch at `:3491-3502`
(`MF7_AIMREFLECT`), reached via `P_ReflectOffActor` (`src/playsim/p_map.cpp:3845-3850`).

Three reasons both mods are right to avoid it, and the survey should say so:
1. **The cone is keyed on the blocker's `Angles.Yaw`** — a body heading, not a blade or a plate
   face. Both mods have a real surface normal and use it.
2. **The deflection is random.** A coin flip between +45° and −45° is the opposite of the skill feel
   both mods are built around (`guard.zs:528-531`, `zscript.txt:2101-2102`).
3. **It is a playsim RNG draw on a path a local decision reaches**, which the owner's standing
   netplay rule forbids. Both mods are deterministic by construction.

**Label: WALL, contraindicated. Do not adopt in either mod. Survey correction required in `07`
§2.2 and §4.4.**

## 2.6 THE TWO-HAND TRAP — a default-on shipped bug, two days old

**The brief asks me to determine whether the Dual-key second blade is an off-hand Weapon and, if
so, to flag a probable shipped bug. It is, and there is one — but not for the reason the brief
expects, and it did not exist a week ago.**

**It is an off-hand `Weapon`.** `UBQS_SaberOff : UBQS_SaberBase` with `+WEAPON.OFFHANDWEAPON`
(`:3046-3053`, flag at `:3050`), brought out by the Dual key (`ubqs_dual_mode` 1, `CVARINFO:40`;
`QuestSaber/KEYCONF`). So a two-handed hilt grip and the second blade are both the trap pose.

**The input half is handled, and handled well.** `GrippedInput()` (`:2173-2194`) is a dedicated
handler whose comment is the clearest statement of the trap I have seen anywhere, the surveys
included:

> *"THE TWO-HAND GRIP TAKES THE OFF HAND'S WEAPON MACHINERY AWAY, so the off-hand saber's own Fire
> state never runs while you hold the hilt with both hands — the single most natural thing to do
> with a lightsaber in a headset. Paint, throw and recall all die; the blade keeps cutting, because
> that runs from DoEffect, so **the failure is partial and silent, which is the worst shape for
> it.**"* — `:2160-2164`

It names the three engine gates correctly (`A_WeaponReady`, `FireWeapon`/`FireWeaponAlt`,
`P_CheckWeaponButtons` — all verified in §1.7) and re-drives paint, throw and recall from
`p.cmd.buttons` for exactly as long as `WF_TWOHANDSTABILIZED` is set, mutually exclusive with the
weapon path (`:2178`). **Nothing to fix there.**

**The render half was handled by the world saber, and on 10-29 the world saber was switched off by
default.** Three flips in the clone that are not in the two-day-old snapshot:

| Cvar | Was | Now | Line |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ubqs_world_saber` | `true` | **`false`** | `CVARINFO:23` — *"the psprite hilt — the default, owner 10-29"* |
| `ubqs_hand_glow` | `0.0` | **`1.0`** | `CVARINFO:79` — *"ON by default (owner 10-29)"* |
| `ubqs_blade_glow` | `0.6` | **`0.0`** | `CVARINFO:14` — *"the WORLD beam strength: off by default"* |

and the code-side fallback moved with them: `WorldUp()` now reads
`UInt("ubqs_world_saber", 0)` (`:1508`, was `…, 1`). So `WorldUp()` returns **false** by default and
`UBQS_WorldSaber` never spawns.

**The off-hand blade is now entirely psprites, and both of its layers are skipped.** Chain, every
link cited:

1. The off hilt is `PSP_OFFHANDWEAPON`; the off beam is overlay layer **2000052**
   (`GlowLayer()`, `:483`), created by `A_Overlay(gl, "GlowHold")` (`:567`).
2. `A_Overlay` creates the layer with `P_CreatePsprite(player, stateowner, layer)` —
   `src/playsim/p_pspr.cpp:1241`. `stateowner` is the weapon whose state ran the action, i.e.
   `UBQS_SaberOff`. (`DPSprite::DPSprite` assigns `Caller = caller`, `p_pspr.cpp:194`.) So the glow
   layer's `Caller` **is** `player->OffhandWeapon`.
3. Both render loops skip exactly that: `if (weaponStabilised && psp->GetCaller() ==
   player->OffhandWeapon) continue;` — `src/rendering/hwrenderer/scene/hw_weapon.cpp:832-835`
   (sprites) and `:917-920` (models).
4. Layer 2000052 is **inside** the skipped loop: the loop runs `psp->GetID() < PSP_TARGETCENTER`
   (`:830`), and `PSP_TARGETCENTER` is `int.max − 2`.
5. The cut is unaffected — `CutAlong` runs from `DoEffect` (`:1847`, called from `:1044+`), which
   stabilisation does not touch.

**Result: with shipped defaults, holding the grip with your hands inside 0.50 m makes the entire
off-hand lightsaber — hilt and blade — vanish, while it goes on cutting and killing.** The
`07` §2.4 escape hatch ("a plain overlay layer survives") **does not apply**, because the escape
depends on the layer's *caller* not being the off-hand weapon, and `A_Overlay` from a weapon's own
state makes it exactly that.

**Severity, honestly.** `weaponStabilised` is written in only two places in the whole engine —
`src/rendering/gl/stereo3d/gl_openvr.cpp:1723` and `:1726` — and the OpenXR device file never
writes it. The owner plays on VDXR/OpenXR, so **this does not fire on his machine today.** It fires
under SteamVR, and `07` §2.4 correction 1 is explicit that "silently broken under SteamVR is still
broken". It is one patch from being live on both paths.

**Fix, two options, both S:**
- **Preferred:** force `WorldUp()` true for the off-hand saber while
  `(player.WeaponState & WF_TWOHANDSTABILIZED) != 0`, regardless of `ubqs_world_saber`. The world
  path is fully present and was the default until two days ago, so this is a one-clause change to a
  tested code path — the same shape as the saw's `(hw != 0 && grabbed)` at `state.zs:862`.
- **Alternative:** give the off beam's overlay layer a caller that is not the off-hand weapon.
  `P_SetPsprite` takes an explicit `newcaller` (`p_pspr.cpp:292-307`, reachable as
  `SetPsprite(id, state, pending, caller)`), so the glow layer could be hosted on a dummy inventory
  item and would then survive the skip. More invasive; it also does not save the **hilt**, which is
  `PSP_OFFHANDWEAPON` and cannot be re-homed.

**And the same hole exists the other way round, as a user setting rather than a default.** Even
after the fix above, a player who sets `ubqs_world_saber 0`… is now every player, which is the
point. Conversely a player who sets it back to 1 is protected. The main-hand saber is **never**
affected: the skip tests `GetCaller() == OffhandWeapon`.

**Label: WALL as shipped → BUILDABLE / S to fix. This is the single most important finding in this
report, and it is two days old. The stale snapshot the brief pointed at still has the old defaults
and would have shown this as "covered by design".**

## 2.7 Edge vs flat, thrust vs slash, cutting through, blade length

| Mechanic | Status |
|---|---|
| **Blade length** | **Modelled.** `BladeLen()` from `ubqs_size` (`CVARINFO:3`) with a separate `ubqs_size_off` for the off blade (`:468`), five baked sizes (`UBQS_Data.Size`, `:30`), and a per-frame extension table for the ignition ramp (`Ext`, `:28`). **EXISTS.** |
| **Thrust vs slash** | **Modelled, and the mechanism is good.** `ubqs_cut_sustain` 0.6 (`CVARINFO:162`) integrates a steady push so a thrust and a draw-cut count, not only fast arcs — the same device the saw uses for its bash (`guard.zs:624-635`). The cut's *direction* comes from the measured arc with the body's motion removed (`sw`, `:1854`), which is what distinguishes a thrust from a slash at resolution time. **EXISTS.** What is **not** modelled is a different *outcome* for the two: a thrust does the same damage as a slash and shoves along its own direction. A thrust-specific result (deeper single-target damage, no shove, no arc) is **BUILDABLE / M** and is a design question for the owner, not a defect. |
| **Cutting through enemies** | **Modelled.** `ubqs_cut_rate` 8 is the per-victim re-cut cooldown and is explicitly *"the real cap on the saber's rate of killing, and the reason holding the blade inside a monster does not delete it"* (`:1867-1868`). One swing through four bodies cuts all four, once each (`cutWho`/`cutWhen`, `:1892-1893`, `:1958-1959`) and buzzes once (`:1965`). **EXISTS.** |
| **Edge vs flat** | **NOT modelled, and I found no attempt.** The cut tests the blade as a **line**: `CutLine` uses only `base` and `dir` (`:1860`), and `BladeDirOf` returns a direction, not a frame. `AttackRoll` *is* read (`HR()`, `:828`) and *is* used — for the blade's roll in `BladeDirOf` and for the twirl — but never to ask whether edge or flat met the target. **BUILDABLE / M**, and for a *lightsaber* arguably correct to omit: a plasma blade has no flat. I would not propose it unless the owner asks. The `back` blade (`:1858`, a double-ended staff) is the shipped use of the second leg and is a more saber-ish idea than edge alignment. |

**Also modelled, beyond the brief's list:** blade-on-blade clash with hysteresis (`:2602-2638`), the
cross-guard block (`Guarding()`, `:2611`, `ubqs_crossguard` 1), wall and floor burn with decals
(`Burn`, `:2575-2596`), and a thrown blade with its own deflection (`UBQS_Thrown`, `:3739`;
`SpinDeflect`, `:4096`).

## 4. SURVEY CORRECTIONS OWED

1. **`WorldHitscanPreFired` exists in `qzd17.3`** and cancels a hitscan before it is traced —
   `src/playsim/p_map.cpp:4718`, `wadsrc/static/zscript/events.zs:168`, `src/events.cpp:704-716`,
   `src/events.h:315`, `:487`. `07` §4.5 and §5 wall 4 call hitscan interception a WALL. Both mods
   ship it. **Relabel EXISTS.**
2. **`+SHIELDREFLECT` scatters by a random ±45° and does not aim at the shooter** —
   `src/playsim/p_mobj.cpp:3468-3477`, `pr_reflect()` at `:3474`. `07` §2.2/§4.4 overstate it.
3. **The two haptic routes are mutually exclusive per hand** —
   `src/rendering/gl/stereo3d/gl_openvr.cpp:519-520`. `00_CAPABILITIES` §1.6a presents them as
   complementary. The 150 ms `LineAttack` pulse (`p_map.cpp:4764-4769`) blacks that hand out for
   ~5 tics and drops queued quake pulses. `01_MELEE` §2 has this right; `00_CAPABILITIES` and `07`
   §3.5 do not.
4. **A hand-held `+SOLID` plate sits inside the player's own collision box at guard pose** and is
   never the actor the missile resolves against — `p_map.cpp:1417-1419`, `:1677-1680`, no distance
   ordering in `PIT_CheckThing`'s iteration. `07` §4.1 should carry the caveat; `guard.zs:3-9`
   records the measurement.
5. **`07` §2.4's "a plain overlay layer survives stabilisation" needs a condition.** It survives
   only if its *caller* is not the off-hand weapon. A layer made with `A_Overlay` from an off-hand
   weapon's own state **has** that caller (`src/playsim/p_pspr.cpp:1241`, `:194`) and is skipped.
   `P_SetPsprite`'s explicit `newcaller` (`:292-307`) is the route that actually escapes. This is
   the distinction the saber's 10-29 regression turns on (§2.6).
6. **`A_WeaponReady`'s stabilisation early-return does not stop the state machine** —
   `wadsrc/static/zscript/actors/inventory/weapons.zs:415-418` returns from `A_WeaponReady`, not
   from the caller's action function, and `WF_TWOHANDSTABILIZED` occurs in exactly nine places in
   the tree, none of which halt psprite state advancement. A state-driven per-tic hook survives the
   trap, which is why the saw's rev, grind and bash keep working. `07` §2.4 implies more is lost
   than is.
